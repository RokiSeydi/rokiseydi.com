Background

My work sits at the intersection of medical anthropology, computational social science, and AI evaluation.

I started with ethnographic research on idioms of distress at King's College London — studying how people navigate health systems in practice, and how much gets lost when that navigation is standardised away. That dissertation was presented at Oxford as the sole undergraduate presenter.

I then trained in global public health at Imperial College London (International Distinction Award), focusing on structural gaps in care delivery and digital adoption.

In parallel, I've built production systems used by millions:

  • Founding backend engineer at Good Boost — digital health platform, 20K+ patients, five industry awards
  • Full stack founding engineer at tell.money — FCA-regulated open banking infrastructure, 5M+ users across major UK banks
  • Founder in Residence at Antler UK — AI-enabled healthcare prototypes and clinical user research

That arc now runs: fieldwork → production systems → computational human context, behavioural agent design, and sociotechnical AI evaluation.
